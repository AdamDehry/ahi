# How we make a talking video

*One page, for the owner and every agent. October 2026.*

**The one idea:** the AI that moves the mouth also says the words, in the same pass, and
we keep that sound. We don't record or generate a voice separately and paste it on top.
Pasting it on is why David's lips were off and why the voice sounded like it was in a
different room. It was.

### Before anything costs money
1. **Write the lines and read them out loud with a timer.** Keep each shot to about two
   to three words per second. Eight seconds holds about 20 words. If it doesn't fit, split
   it into two shots; never squeeze it.
2. **Make the character picture first** and get it approved. A picture costs pennies; a
   talking shot costs 56 credits.
3. **Decide the voice.** Every character gets one short voice sample, 5 to 12 seconds of
   just that voice talking, and that same sample goes into every shot. If the voice
   belongs to a real person, we need their written OK first, and we never use the owner's or
   anyone's voice without it.

### Making each shot
4. **One shot = one short scene, 8 seconds.** Say who is on screen (same words every
   time), what he does second by second, how the camera moves, and then the exact words
   in quotes.
5. **Ask for the room's sound in the same sentence** ("over a quiet sunny street, birds,
   his boots on concrete"). The AI makes it in the same space as the voice. Don't ask for
   music; it fights the lips.
6. **Close and still is best for lips.** Walking and wide shots still work, but the
   cleanest talking is a medium close-up with the camera locked.
7. **Check the price first, every time.** Our rail: one shot over 60 credits stops and
   asks. Do one shot, look at it, then do the rest.

### Checking a shot
8. **Did it say the exact words?** Run a transcript; every word in order.
9. **Do the lips match?** Look at a few words with an "m", "b" or "p" (lips must close)
   and an "oo" or "o" (lips round). If they do, it's synced.
10. **Is it the same voice as the last shot?** Listen back to back. If not, the voice
    sample wasn't attached.

### Finishing
11. **Keep the shot's own sound as the main track.** Add music quietly underneath, so it
    dips whenever someone talks, plus a very quiet steady background so the cuts between
    shots don't jump.
12. **A person listens once before anything goes out.** No machine can tell us it
    sounds right.

### When the shot is perfect but the voice is wrong
Swap the voice with Higgsfield's voice-change tool. It keeps the timing, so the lips
still match. Only if that fails, use LipSync Studio on the Higgsfield website, which a
person has to run. Never paste a new voice over the old one.

### What it costs (Higgsfield, October 2026)
8-second talking shot at 720p: **56 credits**. 480p draft: **24**, and it can be upgraded
to 1080p later. Voice sample: **under 1**. Music bed: **about 2**. A 30-second, three-shot
episode is about **170 credits**.
